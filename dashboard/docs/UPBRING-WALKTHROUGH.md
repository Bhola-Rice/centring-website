# Upbring conversation

**Opening.** “When a grant lands in someone’s inbox, who decides whether it is worth a week of your team’s time? Let’s take one through that decision together.”

Use Lantern Row Heritage Commons, a fictional applicant. Family Studio Saturdays is an invented project with an eight-session plan and a $12,000 budget. Give the audience a concrete question at each stop.

1. **Documents.** Open the starred Family Studio Saturdays brief. Ask where the transport allowance comes from. The $1,600 line is in the source. The hall booking is still open. Those details make it easy to tell what Beedy can draft and what the team needs to confirm.
2. **Opportunities.** Open a grant and compare its applicant rules and purpose with the brief. Ask the grants lead what would rule it out. A weak fit is a useful decision. Use a relevant opportunity or a supplied fictional form for the drafting portion.
3. **Application Studio.** Start a draft with the brief. Read one funder question and its proposed answer side by side with the evidence. Ask the reviewer to correct one sentence. If the funder’s form could not be retrieved, identify provisional sections as provisional.
4. **Team tasks.** Assign the venue confirmation to an owner and set a date. Show how the reviewer can pick up the same work. Stop before submission.

**Closing question.** “If you tried this on one funding round, what would you measure?” Suggest time to a pursue/pass decision, minutes spent locating evidence, and unsupported claims caught before review. Agree on one metric before proposing a pilot.
